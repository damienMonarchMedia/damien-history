PEOPLE · PLACE · PURPOSE

Every place holds
more than one history.

Two lives of service. An inhabited and cultivated ʻāina. A school built through the work of a community.

Follow these intertwined stories from Damien and Edmund Rice back to the lands of Kapālama, then forward to the Monarchs of today.

12 moments   ·   A thematic journey through time

01 / 12   ·   Tremelo, Belgium

1840

A life that would cross oceans.

Joseph de Veuster is born in Belgium on January 3, 1840. Known to the world as Father Damien, his life will become the namesake and moral touchstone of a school half a world away.

02 / 12   ·   Kalaupapa, Molokaʻi

1873

To stand beside others.

Father Damien volunteers to serve the people sent to Kalaupapa under Hawaiʻi’s isolation policies for Hansen’s disease. His example of shared life and service becomes central to the identity of the school that bears his name.

Education as an act of care.

Before 1795

The land already held a story.

Long before classrooms, Kapālama was an inhabited landscape of cultivation and water. Loʻi, springs and ʻauwai sustained communities whose names and relationships remain visible in the surviving land records.

05 / 12   ·   Niuhelewai

c. 1783

A stream, and a difficult memory.

Accounts connect Niuhelewai with the conflict in which Kahekili’s forces overcame Kahahana’s rule on Oʻahu. Remembering this place means attending to violence and loss as well as the water that sustained everyday life.

06 / 12   ·   The Māhele and Land Commission Awards

1848–1854

Names written into the land.

Testimony and surveys preserve the holdings of Kaniniu, Hikiʻau, Waiwaiʻole, Nohunohu and other people in the Kapālama landscape. Their records describe homes, cultivated patches and waterways. An award could contain several separate ʻāpana; it is not the same as a modern tax parcel.

07 / 12   ·   J. F. Brown · Registered Map 1039

1885

A landscape in layers.

Brown’s Kapālama working sheet brings together older award geometry, roads, deeds and estate annotations. Read it as a layered record of title history: its markings do not all describe a single moment in time.

08 / 12   ·   The Houghtailing landscape

1912–13

Before the school, a home.

A surviving photograph shows the Houghtailing plantation house at Houghtailing and Kōhou streets, the present Damien location. Family recollections describe a spring-fed pond, gardens, animals and land used by neighborhood children.

09 / 12   ·   Brother Thomas B. Regan and the founding community

1962

A school built by many hands.

Under founding principal Brother Thomas B. Regan, the Brothers teach while construction continues. Students, families and volunteers help turn the site into a school. Classes begin in a partially completed building: a community taking shape through shared work.
